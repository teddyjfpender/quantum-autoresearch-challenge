# Factored class erasure on Li

## Starting point and hypothesis

This circuit builds on `li/kl-l4hfge-m8` with the same four-group split one-hot, eight outer and eight inner keep bits, and block settings. The parent was the Li score leader in the checked-out ledger: 17,777.483 Toffolis per step, 471 peak logical qubits, score 8,373,194.493. The hypothesis was that the class one-hot's measured erasure need not recreate every initial row flag. Once its slots have collapsed, the four class flags and three group flags are a factored description of the row index. Recreating all twelve nonfirst flags of rows 0-15 charges twelve Toffolis per copy, even though the index bits already identify them. Rows 0-14 are square; row 15 is a full one-body row, and row 16 is the grafted one-body tail.

## Mechanism and correctness

Extension lever `.c` affects only the final class erasure for the four-by-four initial-row layout of this Li point. It is guarded by exact shape assertions and leaves every recorded build unchanged. Following the existing slot collapse and graft restoration, it X-measures the four class flags and three group flags. Their measurement phases are corrected with the row-index bits. For four possible values of two selector bits, the outcome-selected predicate has algebraic normal form `m0 + (m0 xor m1)x + (m0 xor m2)y + (m0 xor m1 xor m2 xor m3)xy` over GF(2). Multiplying by the indicator of rows 0-15 gives Z and CZ corrections plus only the final cubic CCZ term, conditioned on the parity of the four classical measurement outcomes. The three group flags use the same formula with `m0=0`. The grafted tail subrows then collapse through the existing measured binary splits; their root is erased with a Z correction on row 16's index bit.

For every basis lane, the measured flags are exact functions of the still-coherent row index. Each H-basis measurement contributes a sign equal to its outcome times that function; the polynomial applies precisely the same sign and cancels it. The new gadget test exhausts the 17 possible rows under 16 measurement seeds. Its mutant omits the cubic correction and is detected. A separate 14-orbital exact-spec test forces the same four-by-four class geometry, passes the trusted evaluator, and confirms that the trusted evaluator rejects the cubic-phase mutant. The full challenge evaluators are the authority for the integrated Li circuit.

## Measurements and research log

The parent's static cost is 17,777.5 Toffolis at 471 qubits. With `.c`, the static ledger reports 17,755.5 at 471, a reduction of 22 Toffolis per step and a score estimate of 8.3628 million. These are local estimates, not a judge result. The change targets `copy: RPREP^dagger angle erasure`; it composes with the parent's paired item lookup, folded read, grafted class layout, partial angle unloading, and other levers.

I also screened the adjacent five-group Li point. Its 187 slots are already at the information lower bound `ceil(931/5)`, and nearby class-depth and rank-schedule settings did not beat the Li leader after qubit cost. Reducing inner keep bits lowered the raw count but violates the pinned estimated-rounding rule, so it was discarded. Changing lookup block counts did not help because the four-group and five-group points are limited by different stages. The first seven-orbital test layout had no spare cell after the square-row identity stand-in; a fourteen-orbital layout reproduced the Li geometry. Its 20-bit alias precision missed the rigorous small-spec rounding bound, so the test uses 22 bits. I checked the [OpenAI mathematics catalogue](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) for quantum circuit results. Its relevant entries concern asymptotic complexity or different circuit models, with no justified immediate substitution for this exact measured erasure. The proposed erasure is a refinement of `onehot-split`, not a new architecture.
